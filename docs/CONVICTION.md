# The conviction layer — how ProSignal decides 0, 1 or 2

> Status: **wired and live**. Every threshold is **UNVALIDATED**. The
> selection-precision study that would calibrate them has not been run, and
> until it has, the conviction grade orders candidates against each other and
> claims nothing about what happens next.

---

## What was wrong

The engine ranked hundreds of names well and then decided what to buy with two
things that have nothing to do with evidence.

**A calendar.** `stage6_entry.admission.entry_cadence_sessions = 21`, anchored
at 2026-09-01. On 20 of every 21 sessions the engine *could not* issue a BUY
whatever it found. Measured on a live run: 36 names cleared every evidence gate
and the answer was NO TRADE, because it was session 2 of 21.

**A rank band.** `model_rank <= entry_rank (6)`. Being first was treated as
evidence of being ahead.

Everything that was *supposed* to make the output scarce did nothing:

| control | value | what it actually did |
|---|---|---|
| `min_composite_score` | 0.60 | cut **0 of 36** |
| `min_universe_percentile` | 90.0 | cut **0 of 36** — and it is the same test: the score is `(rank-1)/(n-1)`, uniform every session by construction, so no absolute threshold on it can ever bind |
| `min_dispersion_ratio` | 0.50 | reads `prediction_dispersion`, a field only the deleted fitted model populated — **silently skipped** by an `is not None` guard |
| `min_win_probability` | 0.0 | disabled; its classifier measured per-date AUC 0.4996 |
| `cash_rule` | on | fires on **one session in eight years** |

So scarcity was a property of the calendar, and the calendar was the only thing
deciding. Meanwhile the *visible* output was 33–37 WATCHLIST cards per session,
each with a full trade plan.

One more finding, from the ledger: all 251 replay runs are dated 2023-10-26 to
2026-08-25 — every one of them **before** the cadence anchor, where
`cadence.resolve` returns "the schedule has not started, entries are OPEN". The
recorded history was generated by a strategy that buys every session; the live
engine bought on one in 21. `cadence.py`'s own docstring calls those "different
strategies, not the same strategy sampled differently."

## What replaced it

`stage9_conviction` runs after Stage 7 (it needs the risk plans — the economic
gate prices the actual position) and before Stage 8 (which builds the cards).
When it is enabled the rank band and the entry clock are **silent**.

```
every Stage 5 survivor
    │
    ├─ shortlist by post-defence score        (compute bound, not a rule)
    │
    ├─ INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE     how many bets is this really?
    ├─ SEPARATION               is it ahead, or merely first?
    ├─ ROBUSTNESS               does it survive other defensible weightings?
    ├─ ECONOMICS                does the edge survive its own cost?
    ├─ MOMENTUM-CRASH DEFENCE   is this a momentum bet in a panic state?
    │
    ├─ slot 1   (best decision quality among names that cleared everything)
    │
    ├─ INDEPENDENCE             is #2 a second bet, or the same one twice?
    └─ slot 2
```

Every stage can end in nothing.

### Independent evidence — the one that binds hardest

Meucci's Effective Number of Bets, applied to evidence rather than capital.
Given the candidate's exposure `w` over its *supporting* factors and the
correlation matrix `S` of those factors **measured on today's own
cross-section**:

```
d_i = (Eᵀw)_i² · λ_i / (wᵀ S w)          where  S = E diag(λ) Eᵀ
ENB = exp( −Σ d_i ln d_i )
```

`d` is a probability distribution over the uncorrelated principal directions of
the evidence; ENB is the exponential of its Shannon entropy, bounded in `[1, n]`.
ENB = 1 means one bet wearing *n* hats.

**Measured on the live cross-section:**

| rank | ticker | supporting factors | independent directions | ratio | largest direction |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | SKYGOLD | 14 | **1.53** | 9.1× | 91% |
| 2 | MBAPL | 14 | 2.16 | 6.5× | 80% |
| 3 | HFCL | 11 | 1.93 | 5.7× | 84% |
| 8 | SJS | 14 | 2.32 | 6.0× | 67% |
| 11 | TI | 14 | **3.24** | 4.3× | 59% |

The top-ranked name's card listed fourteen confirmations. There were
effectively **one and a half**. It was refused. The name that was bought sat at
rank 2 — which is the whole point of §55: *top 2 only after quality*, never
"take the top 2 and justify them".

Note rank 11: broader evidence than rank 1. That is the alpha-vs-conviction
distinction being visible in real data.

### Separation

Measured on `composite_raw`, **never** on the rank, and divided by the
cross-section's own robust dispersion (MAD × 1.4826) so gaps are comparable
across days:

```
gap_to(k) = (raw_1 − raw_k) / σ_raw
```

The no-edge zone is real and visible: on the live run, ranks 7–10 were
separated by **0.004 to 0.013 σ**. That ordering is noise, and the gate says so.

### Robustness

The universe is re-ranked under 16 alternative specifications — each theme
dropped, each tilted ±25%, and equal-weight — and the candidate's survival rate
inside the top 10 is recorded. Weights are perturbed **per name**, because the
shipped blend re-caps over the themes each name actually has.

Lookbacks are *not* perturbed here: that means recomputing factors from prices
for the whole universe, which is a Stage 4 rerun per specification. It belongs
in the offline study. Theme weights are where the estimation error lives.

### Economics — the number that makes it bind

`docs/RESULTS_OF_RECORD.json` measures the ranking's top-decile excess:

| horizon | top-decile excess | corrected t |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 21 | +0.64% | 2.49 |
| 42 | +1.20% | 2.36 |
| 63 | +1.76% | 2.21 |

The shipped cost model prices real live candidates at **60–84 bps round-trip**.
So:

- **at 21 sessions the measured edge is smaller than the cost of a typical
  candidate.** The trade is negative before it starts.
- at 63 sessions — the shipped horizon — a candidate costing 84 bps keeps about
  92 bps of a 176 bps edge.

What is computed is a **ratio**, not a forecast: *"this trade spends 47% of the
only edge the model has ever demonstrated."* The engine has no calibrated
per-name expected return and this layer does not invent one. The reference is
read from the results file rather than hardcoded, so the constant and the
measurement cannot drift apart.

### Momentum-crash defence

Daniel & Moskowitz (2016): momentum crashes are partly forecastable, cluster
after market declines in high volatility, and land on the rebound. A candidate
whose case is ≥50% momentum is **refused** in a panic state (declining trend
with VIX ≥ 70th percentile of its trailing year, or a regime transition at that
volatility). Outside a panic state the momentum share is disclosed, not
penalised.

### Independence — the second slot must be earned

Three reads, because they fail differently:

1. **Price.** Return correlation, then correlation of the **residuals after
   removing the equal-weight market**. Raw correlation rejects good pairs in a
   trending market and accepts bad ones in a flat one.
2. **Evidence.** Cosine similarity of the two names' theme-contribution
   vectors. Two names carried by momentum alone are one bet on momentum.
3. **Basket.** ENB of the two-name book. 2.00 is two bets; 1.00 is one position
   held twice.

If independence cannot be **measured**, the second slot stays empty. An
unmeasured correlation is not a low one.

## What it does across history

91 sessions, every 21st from 2019-01-01 to 2026-08-28, no errors:

| BUYs | sessions | share |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 38 | 41.8% |
| 1 | 29 | 31.9% |
| 2 | 24 | 26.4% |

0.85 BUYs per scanned session. 1,147 candidates evaluated, 99 cleared (8.6%),
77 bought. All three output states occur naturally; none was tuned for.

**The 38 NO-TRADE days are not one thing**, and the distinction matters:

| cause | sessions |
|---|---:|
| no candidate cleared conviction | 24 |
| market-wide safety halt (Stage 5) | 12 |
| universe insufficient | 2 |

**CORRECTION.** An earlier draft of this document claimed the zeros through the
March–May 2020 crash were the momentum-crash defence firing. They were not.
Those sessions show `evaluated=0` — Stage 9 returned early on Stage 5's
market-wide halt, which is pre-existing machinery. The crash-window behaviour is
correct, but it is not this layer's doing and must not be credited to it.

**Three of the five gates are nearly inert.** Of 315 sampled refusals:

| gate | share of refusals |
|---|---:|
| independent evidence (ENB) | 78.4% |
| separation from the next candidate | 21.3% |
| specification robustness | 0.3% |
| evidence concentration | 0% |
| net cost burden | 0% |

This is the same pathology the old engine had — a stack of gates where one does
all the work — and it is now a property of THIS layer. One unvalidated
threshold, `min_independent_evidence = 2.0`, is deciding almost every refusal.
The cost gate never binds because live candidates run 28–50% cost burden
against a 60% cap. Both facts are inputs to the threshold study, not defects to
be patched by moving numbers until the shares look even.

**The grade scale only ever emits two of its four values**: 39 B+ and 38 A−
across 77 BUYs, never A, never B. `quality()` sums components the gates have
already floored, so its live range is narrow by construction. The grade orders
candidates within a day, which is all it was ever entitled to do, but the
A/A−/B+/B ladder is not calibrated and currently is not even fully used.

```bash
prosignal research conviction --start 2019-01-01 --stride 21 --out book.json
```

reports the BUY-frequency distribution, which gate bound on each refusal, and
the breakdown by regime.

## What is NOT claimed

- **The grade is not a probability.** `A / A− / B+ / B` is ordinal. No mapping
  from grade to realised outcome frequency exists on this engine.
- **The thresholds are not validated.** They are a priori choices. The
  selection-precision study — top 1/2/3/5/10/20, per date then averaged, net of
  the shipped cost model — is what moves them.
- **Concentration is not evidenced.** `RESULTS_OF_RECORD` measures the shipped
  six-name book at **−17.4% annualised gross** against its own universe, and
  the ranking's edge over equal-weight collapses from +1.16% to +0.10% once
  fitted on the tradeable population (`docs/REAUDIT.md`). Going to two names
  moves *further* into the region where this engine has no demonstrated edge.
  The conviction layer is a better way to choose them; it is not evidence that
  choosing two is right.

That last point is the open question the next phase has to answer.

The conviction record — **every candidate evaluated, including the refused
ones** — is written to `LedgerRow.conviction` on every run. That is the research
substrate: without the refused half, the question "did the names the gate turned
down go on to beat the ones it took?" is unanswerable.
