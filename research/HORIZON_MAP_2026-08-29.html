Where ProSignal’s predictive information actually lives

An independent rebuild of the engine’s panel from raw price data, scored on 1,912 daily signal dates and measured at eleven forecast horizons — with every result pushed through overlap correction, deflation for multiple testing, realistic Indian delivery-segment costs, and a deliberate attempt to falsify the strongest finding.

ProSignal contains real, tradable cross-sectional information — but almost none of it is contributed by the fitted composite. The signal is a momentum-family ranking engine whose economics live at 10–126 sessions; the estimator that combines its factors currently subtracts value at every horizon tested.

01

What was measured, and how

Nothing here is quoted from the engine’s own research files. The panel was rebuilt from the raw store — ten years of NSE bhavcopy, delivery, corporate actions, index and sector data — and every factor was re-implemented from the source definitions in crosssec.py and crossmodel.py. The point-in-time universe screen was reproduced exactly: EQ series, 60-session median turnover ≥ ₹5 Cr, quoted price ≥ ₹20, ≥ 300 sessions of listed history, capped at 750 names by turnover, with the price floor applied to the unadjusted close so no name is excluded for a split nobody had announced.

Two data facts that shape everything downstream

Delivery data begins 2019-06-27 and only reaches full coverage in 2020. The delivery family — the theme carrying the highest t-statistic in the live model — is structurally blank for the first sixth of the panel and ranks every name neutral there.

Six source files and config/parameters.yaml currently carry unresolved git conflict markers (stage4_core_score.py, crosssec.py, crossmodel.py, validation/metrics.py, harness.py, forward.py). As it sits on disk the package does not import. That is why this study re-implements rather than calls the engine — and it means any number produced by the repo in its current state should be re-derived before it is trusted.

02

The horizon map

The first thing to separate is information from return. Rank information coefficient rises monotonically with horizon for every momentum-shaped signal in this engine and keeps rising to 189 sessions. Annualised return does not. Reading the IC curve alone nominates the longest horizon available; reading the net-of-cost curve nominates something between ten and a hundred and twenty-six sessions. The gap between them is turnover, and it is the whole game.

03

The finding that explains the rest: the alpha is entirely in the right tail

ObservedSignificantRobustMechanism

In this universe the cross-sectional return distribution is violently right-skewed — skewness of the cross-sectional excess return runs +1.87 at 63 sessions and +4.85 at one. That single fact reconciles two results that otherwise look contradictory, and it is the reason the engine underperforms its own inputs.

For every signal at every horizon tested, the median name in the top decile underperforms the cross-sectional mean, while the mean of that decile beats it. At H=126, a top-decile name selected by plain 6-1 momentum has a median excess return of −1.79% and a mean of +3.77%. The fraction of top-decile names that beat the cross-section is 46–48% for every signal in the study — barely above the 42–45% base rate that right-skew imposes on any random name.

Why this is the central design finding

ProSignal fits against label_rank — the cross-sectional rank of the forward outcome — and gates themes on the t-statistic of a rank regression. A rank objective optimises the ordering of the median outcome. In a distribution this skewed, the ordering of medians is close to orthogonal to where the money is. The estimator is being asked to solve a problem adjacent to the one that pays.

This is testable rather than rhetorical. Refitting the identical seven families, on the identical walk-forward schedule, changing only the regression target from cross-sectional rank to cross-sectional excess return, moves the model from negative to positive top-decile alpha at every label horizon:

Read down the last column. Every rank-target specification is economically dead. Every return-target specification is alive. This is the single highest-leverage change available to the engine, and it costs one line in fit_coefficients.

The shipped composite’s decile profile is the profile of an avoid list, not a buy list. Its first decile runs −7.1% annualised at H=21; its tenth runs −2.8%. Whatever the score knows, it knows it about the bottom of the distribution.

04

The composite is worse than the parts it is built from

ObservedRobustEconomic

Three things happen inside the estimator, and each of them costs money.

The gate goes dark for years at a time. Walking the significance gate forward, no theme clears |t| ≥ 2 on 651 of 1,912 dates — the engine cannot rank at all on 34% of the sample. Almost all of 2021 and 2022 is blank. Under the analytic overlap correction (the more honest standard error, and the one the engine’s own source argues for) coverage falls to 47% of dates.

The themes it does price are not the themes that pay. Across the walk-forward, delivery is priced on 967 of 1,261 scored dates and mom on 799. Delivery has the highest t-statistic in the panel and among the weakest economics: its top-decile excess is +2.3% annualised at H=10 falling to +1.4% at H=63, and after market, size, volatility and beta controls its long-short alpha is +2.2% p.a. at t = 0.90 — indistinguishable from zero.

Ranking on a rank target picks low-dispersion names. The clearest demonstration is the low-volatility control built for this study: sorting on inverse idiosyncratic volatility produces a rank IC of +0.034 at t = 3.0 — and a top-decile return of −4.5% annualised. Positive information, negative money, for exactly the reason in §03.

05

Factor × horizon: the engine contains at least two signals with opposite clocks

ObservedSignificantMechanism

Decomposing the score into the seven families the engine actually fits shows why one horizon cannot serve it. The families do not share a clock.

  • Momentum (mom, 6-1, prox-52w, residual) — IC rises monotonically from +0.009 to +0.088 across the horizon grid; annualised return is flat. A slow state variable. Optimal economic horizon 21–126 sessions.
  • Reversal — IC −0.016 at one session, −0.018 at five, crossing zero near 63 and reaching +0.028 at 189. Two different economic phenomena inside one column.
  • Lottery — IC −0.037 at one session, significant to H=21, then decaying. Useful only as an exclusion; its long-short spread is zero at every horizon.
  • Delivery — the sharpest short-horizon decay in the set: top-decile excess +4.3% annualised at H=1, +2.3% at H=10, ≈0 at H=21. Fitting it against a 63-session label discards most of what it knows.
  • Beta and drawdown — no economically meaningful contribution at any horizon; drawdown’s apparent IC is momentum wearing a risk label (max-drawdown rank correlates +0.63 with 52-week proximity by the engine’s own measurement).

The shapes answer the entry-timing question directly. 6-1 momentum keeps accruing for a full year — +0.5% by 21 sessions, +2.0% by 63, +4.4% by 126, +10.5% by 252, with the annualised rate rising rather than falling. That is a positioning signal. Residual momentum front-loads: +10.5% annualised over the first session, decaying to +6.5% by a year. That is an entry-timing signal with a long tail. Delivery peaks at four days and is negative by a year. The shipped composite goes negative after 21 sessions and reaches −5.9% cumulative by 252.

Cross-sectional ranking, not direction

ObservedRobust

The two questions — will this stock rise? and will this stock beat its peers? — were tested separately. The engine is measurably better at the second, and both edges are small.

Against a base rate of 43.7%, the composite’s bottom decile beats the cross-section only 39.5% of the time — a −4.2 pp discrimination — while its top decile manages +2.0 pp. This is the same "avoid list" structure the decile profile showed, now in probability terms.

What this rules out

ProSignal is not a directional timing engine. A +2.6 pp lift in P(up) at a quarterly horizon is inside the noise of the market’s own drift, and the engine has no mechanism that would make it one — every input is a cross-sectional rank. Used as a market-neutral or peer-relative ranker it has something; used to answer "will this go up" it has almost nothing.

07

Does more confidence mean more edge?

ObservedSignificant

For the momentum-family signals, yes, and cleanly — the top 5% of the cross-section carries roughly three times the excess return of the 80th–95th percentile band, and the relationship is monotone across all six buckets at every horizon. For the shipped composite, no: its highest-conviction bucket is worse than the one below it at every horizon beyond 21 sessions.

The composite’s p95–100 band returns +5.1% annualised at H=1 but −0.4% at H=63 and −0.6% at H=126 — below its own p80–95 band (+2.7% and +1.5%). Its strongest signals do not hold up over the horizon it was fitted for. Momentum’s p95–100 band runs +12.7% / +11.4% / +6.7% / +8.2% / +9.5% across H = 1/5/21/63/126, monotone above every other band throughout.

Calibration

The engine emits a 0–1 composite score and a strength_band label. Neither is calibrated to anything. Sorting the cross-section into twentieths by score and measuring the realised probability of beating the cross-sectional median at H=21 gives a range of 44.0% to 52.8% — a total spread of 8.8 percentage points across the entire score range, slope +0.070 per unit of percentile. The relationship is monotone and real; it is also so shallow that a "High" strength band is, empirically, a 52.8% coin.

08

Regime and conditioning: where the composite breaks

ObservedRobust

Regimes were defined objectively and in advance of measurement: trend from Nifty 200 against its own 200-day average, volatility from India VIX terciles, dispersion from the cross-sectional standard deviation of 21-day returns in terciles, and a twelve-month market-direction split.

The composite returns −15.8% annualised at H=63 in downtrends, −16.4% in the top VIX tercile, −14.2% in twelve-month bear markets, and −9.9% in high-dispersion regimes. It is positive only in uptrend / low-volatility states, where it earns +2.7% to +10.5%. It is a fair-weather signal. Delivery — its largest coefficient — has the same shape: negative in downtrends and high volatility.

Residual momentum is the exception, and it is the most valuable robustness result in the study. It is positive in every regime at every horizon: +12.6% annualised at H=1 in downtrends, +10.6% in the high-VIX tercile, +18.7% in twelve-month bear markets. Raw momentum has the classic crash exposure (≈0 at H=21–63 in downtrends); the residual construction does not — which is exactly what Blitz, Huij & Martens argue the residual is for.

Size, liquidity, volatility, sector

Conditioning on ADTV terciles, trailing-volatility terciles, price terciles and sector shows no concentration that would make the momentum result an artifact:

  • Liquidity — the momentum family returns +5.3% / +6.4% / +7.0% annualised at H=1 across large / mid / small ADTV terciles, and +4.0% / +5.1% / +6.1% at H=63. A mild small-cap tilt, not a dependence. The composite is negative in the large-ADTV tercile at every horizon — the only tercile it could be traded at scale in.
  • Volatility — momentum works in all three terciles (+5.3% to +8.7% at H=1); residual momentum likewise (+9.6% to +12.8%).
  • Price — the strongest returns sit in the low-price tercile (+13.1% for momentum at H=1 against +2.8% for high-price), which is a real implementation caveat: the cheapest names are also the ones where the ₹20 floor and the impact model bite hardest.
  • Sector — top-20 book sector Herfindahl is 0.079–0.087 for every signal, with Financial Services at 15–18% (its weight in the universe). No sector bet.

Economic

Costs were built from the engine’s own configured Indian delivery-segment schedule rather than assumed. The statutory floor is fixed and unavoidable; the variable part is spread and impact.

Turnover was measured, not modelled: the fraction of a 20-name book replaced at each rebalance, averaged over every phase.

Holding-period optimisation

Rather than picking the highest Sharpe, the question is where the marginal benefit of holding longer turns negative. Reading the net curves:

  • Below 5 sessions, nothing survives. Every signal in the study — including the ones with the highest gross Sharpe — is net-negative at H=1 under base costs. Residual momentum at H=1 has a gross long-short Sharpe of 1.69 on a 20-name book and a net return of −12.5%. The one-day information is real and inaccessible.
  • 5 to 10 sessions is where the fast signals become viable and where residual momentum peaks net (+8.7% long, +4.0% long-short at H=10).
  • 21 to 63 sessions is the plateau. Net long-short return on 6-1 momentum runs +7.3% / +9.7% / +11.4% at H=21 / 42 / 63 and flattens at +10.9% to +11.7% thereafter. The marginal benefit of holding past 63 sessions is roughly zero.
  • Past 126 sessions the alpha is unchanged and the sample is thin — 10 to 15 independent observations. Longer is not better; it is merely cheaper and less well measured.

The economic horizon and the statistical horizon are not the same

Rank IC peaks at 189 sessions. Deflated Sharpe peaks at 63–126. Net-of-cost Sharpe peaks at 63. Gross Sharpe peaks at 5–10. The intersection that survives all four is 21–63 sessions for the momentum family — which happens to bracket, and slightly undercut, the engine’s configured 63.

10

What survives honest statistics

SignificantRobust

Overlapping observations

Daily signal dates with an H-session forward return share H−1 sessions of every neighbour’s window. Every t-statistic here uses Newey–West with the analytic variance-inflation factor as a floor — the arithmetic inflation the sampling scheme implies, rather than an estimate of it, because at small n the estimator recovers roughly half the true value. Point estimates use all phases of the daily series; the single-phase non-overlapping t is computed as a cross-check and agrees to within 0.3 in every case reported. Independent-observation counts are stated everywhere: at H=189 there are ten.

Deflated Sharpe ratio

This study ran 416 signal × horizon × construction trials. Charging that count globally is punitive and mixes horizons whose per-period Sharpe is not comparable, so the DSR below is computed within each horizon’s own trial family, which is the fair comparison.

  • Residual momentum peaks at DSR = 0.95 at H=10 (0.94 at H=5, 0.89 at H=21, 0.68 at H=63). The strongest deflation-resistant result in the study.
  • 6-1 momentum reaches 0.68 at H=63 and 0.72 at H=84.
  • Equal-weight family composite reaches 0.67–0.68 at H=42–84.
  • The shipped composite peaks at 0.15 at H=3 and falls to 0.04 by H=63. It does not survive deflation at any horizon.
  • Delivery peaks at 0.15. Reversal at 0.08. Neither survives.

The honest reading of the split

The full-sample verdict on the composite is dominated by its first third. Restricted to 2024-01-24 onward — 640 sessions, ~30 independent 21-day periods — the shipped composite earns +11.2% gross and +3.6% net annualised at H=5, +4.1% net at H=10, +2.9% at H=21 and +2.4% at H=63, at a gross Sharpe of 1.06–1.11 in the first two. That is a real, if modest, live-shaped result and it deserves to be stated as clearly as the failure.

And momentum has weakened over exactly the same window. 6-1 momentum is ~0% at H=21 in the recent third; residual momentum falls from +12.6% to +9.8% at H=1 and from +13.6% to +3.9% at H=63. The edge that dominates the full sample is not the edge that dominates the last two and a half years. Delivery is: it goes from −6.2% to +6.0% at H=21 across the same split.

Two readings are consistent with this and the data cannot separate them: either the engine has genuinely learned something (its delivery-heavy fit is aligned with the regime that arrived), or 2.5 years and ~30 independent periods is too short to distinguish learning from luck. The second is the safer prior — but the first is live and testable, and §14 says how.

Failure modes — stated explicitly

Where the engine does not work, in order of how much it costs.

  1. Below five sessions, nothing is collectable. The strongest gross short-horizon results in the study — residual momentum at a Sharpe of 1.52, short-term reversal at +8.6% annualised on the top decile — are destroyed by 30 book turns a year. Reversal is the cleanest example: real information (IC −0.016 at t = −7.4), zero net return under any cost scenario.
  2. The composite has no long side. Top-decile excess is negative at every horizon over the full sample; the only reliable structure is a deep first decile. As a buy list it is not working; as an exclusion filter it is.
  3. The composite fails in stress. −15.8% annualised at H=63 in downtrends, −16.4% in the top VIX tercile, −14.2% in twelve-month bear markets. It is positive only in uptrend/low-volatility states.
  4. It fails in the liquid part of the universe. Negative top-decile excess in the large-ADTV tercile at every horizon — the only tercile with real capacity.
  5. The significance gate blanks a third of the sample. 651 of 1,912 dates carry no priced theme. Under the analytic overlap correction, 53%.
  6. The score is not a probability. Full-range spread of 8.8 pp in P(beat median). A "High" band is a 52.8% coin.
  7. Its highest-conviction signals decay fastest. The p95–100 band goes negative beyond 21 sessions while p80–95 stays positive.
  8. The P&L is tail-dependent. Delete the best 1% of stock-periods and the composite’s H=63 result turns negative.
  9. Value and quality are unmeasured. Both families drop on the 60% date-span rule; the fundamentals feed reaches back only to 2023-06 against a panel starting 2018-11. Eleven independent 63-session windows is not an estimate.
  10. Delivery — the largest live coefficient — has no alpha after controls (t = 0.90 at H=21, t = 0.44 at H=63) and no data before mid-2019.
  11. The forecast horizon and the exit geometry disagree by a factor of twenty. A 63-session label; a 3-session median hold.
14

Options — how to get the most out of this engine

Ranked by expected improvement per unit of work, each with the measurement that supports it and what it would cost to be wrong. None of these require new data.

  1. Change the fitted target from rank to excess return

    Evidence: identical factors, identical walk-forward — rank targets produce −2.5% to +1.4% top-decile excess and are net-negative at every horizon; return targets produce +0.3% to +7.0% and are net-positive from H=5 outward. The 5-session return target is the best fitted specification in the study (+4.7% net at H=126, +3.8% at H=63).

    Mechanism: the cross-sectional return distribution has skewness +1.9 to +4.9. A rank objective orders medians; the money is in the mean. Cost of being wrong: low — it is a one-line change to fit_coefficients and it is directly testable against the existing holdout.

  2. Stop trading the fitted composite as a long book; keep it as an exclusion filter

    Evidence: DSR ≤ 0.15 at every horizon, FDR q = 0.888 on top-decile return, PBO 0.861 on the horizon choice, negative mean after deleting 1% of observations. Meanwhile its first decile runs −7.1% annualised at H=21 with an entirely stable sign, and its bottom-decile relative hit rate (39.5% against a 43.7% base) is its sharpest discrimination.

    What to do instead: use the composite to remove the bottom two deciles from a book selected on momentum. Cost of being wrong: low; it is a screen, not a position.

  3. Run the book on residual momentum at 10–21 sessions, and 6-1 momentum at 63

    Evidence: residual momentum — DSR 0.95 at H=10, FDR q = 0.0003, alpha +6.6% p.a. at t = 3.35 after a hostile long-short momentum control, positive in every regime including downtrends and high-VIX. 6-1 momentum — alpha +15.1% p.a. at t = 5.81 at H=63, net +11.4% long-short, PBO 0.000, survives deletion of the top 1% of contributions.

    Cost of being wrong: moderate. Both weakened in the last third of the sample (see §11); size accordingly.

  4. Split the engine into two sleeves on different clocks instead of one score at one horizon

    Evidence: reversal’s IC crosses from −0.018 at H=5 to +0.028 at H=189; delivery peaks at four days and is negative at a year; momentum keeps accruing for 252 sessions. Averaging factors with opposite clocks into one 63-session score cancels both.

    Caveat, measured: a naive 50/50 blend of a fast reversal sleeve and a slow momentum sleeve is worse than the momentum sleeve alone (+3.0% net at H=63 against +11.7%) because the fast sleeve’s turnover eats the book. Sleeves must be separately sized and separately costed, not blended into one ranking. Cost of being wrong: moderate — this is a real architecture change.

  5. Reconcile the exit geometry with the forecast horizon

    Evidence: 63-session label, 3-session median realised hold, 75% of trades closed inside 15 sessions. The engine is forecasting one thing and trading another.

    Two coherent choices: either widen the stop/target so the median hold approaches 21–63 sessions and collect what the model forecasts, or refit the model against a 5-session return target (option 1) so the forecast matches the book. Both are defensible; the current pairing is not. Cost of being wrong: low — this is measurable in the existing ledger within a quarter.

  6. Replace the |t| ≥ 2 cliff with continuous shrinkage

    Evidence: the gate blanks 34% of dates (53% under the analytic standard error), and the theme sitting on it — lottery — flips inclusion on an implementation detail worth 0.6 of a t-statistic. A hard gate on a noisy statistic makes the traded coefficient a step function of noise.

    Cost of being wrong: low. The engine already implements the taper (significance_taper, taper_c); it is switched off.

  7. Trade the equal-weight family composite as the benchmark the fit must beat

    Evidence: no fitting at all — a simple average of the seven family ranks — delivers +10.0% annualised top-decile excess at H=10 (t = 3.40), net +7.9%, DSR 0.67 at H=42, FDR q = 0.019. The fitted model has never beaten it out of sample in this study.

    Use: as the null the estimator has to clear before it is allowed to size a position. Cost of being wrong: none — it is a benchmark.

  8. Weight the book toward the top 5%, not the top decile

    Evidence: the p95–100 band earns +12.7% / +8.9% / +7.9% annualised at H=1 / 21 / 126 for residual momentum against +5.0% / +4.0% / +3.9% for p80–95 — roughly 2.5× the edge, monotone at every horizon. The relationship holds for every momentum-family signal and fails only for the shipped composite.

    Cost of being wrong: moderate — concentration raises the tail dependence documented in §10.

  9. Fix the data and code hygiene that currently blocks reproduction

    Six modules and the config file carry unresolved merge conflicts; the package does not import as it stands. Delivery is blank before 2019-06. Value and quality are fitted on 11 independent windows. None of these is a research finding, but each one caps how much any research finding can be trusted.

What would most reduce the remaining uncertainty

One experiment, cheap, decisive: refit the existing model against a 5-session excess-return target and run it forward, unmodified, alongside the current rank-target model, for six months. Both score the same universe on the same dates; the only difference is the objective. The study predicts a gap of roughly 4–6 percentage points of annualised top-decile excess. Six months at daily scoring gives ~25 independent 5-session periods — enough to see a gap that size if it is real, and cheap enough that being wrong costs nothing but the compute.

15

Verdict

A · What ProSignal is actually good at

It is a cross-sectional ranking engine over a momentum-dominated factor set, and its usable information is concentrated in two places: the momentum family, whose economics run from about 10 to about 126 sessions, and the bottom of its own composite ranking, which identifies underperformers more sharply than it identifies outperformers. It is not a directional timing engine (+2.6 pp on P(up) at a quarter), not an intraday or one-day engine (all short-horizon information is consumed by turnover), and not — on the full sample — a working long-only stock picker in its current fitted form.

B · Optimal horizon

21–63 trading days for the momentum-family signal the engine already computes, with the net-of-cost optimum at 63 and the deflated-Sharpe optimum at 10–21 for the residual-momentum variant. The engine’s configured 63 is defensible and sits on a genuine plateau — the choice of 63 is not the problem. Below 5 sessions no horizon survives costs. Above 126 the alpha is unchanged and the sample is 10–15 independent observations.

C · Confidence

HIGH — momentum-family edge at 21–63d HIGH — the composite adds no value over its inputs MEDIUM — the rank-target diagnosis LOW — the recent-period improvement

HIGH that the momentum-family edge at 21–63 sessions is real: 1,912 signal dates, 30–91 independent observations, alpha t = 5.81 after four factor controls, DSR 0.68, PBO 0.000, positive in every chronological third and every liquidity tercile, survives deletion of the top 1% of contributions, and a random-ranking control behaves correctly throughout.

HIGH that the fitted composite currently subtracts value relative to its own inputs: it is worse than an unweighted average of the same seven columns on every economic measure at every horizon, over the full sample.

MEDIUM on the rank-target diagnosis. The experiment is clean and the mechanism is measured, but it is one experiment on one panel; the effect size (4–6 pp) is estimated, not confirmed out of sample.

LOW on the recent-period improvement. 2.5 years, ~30 independent 21-day periods, and the split was made after seeing the full-sample result. It is a hypothesis, not a finding.

D · Strongest evidence

Residual momentum, long-short, at H=5–10: annualised Sharpe 1.71–1.75, deflated Sharpe 0.94–0.95 within its trial family, FDR q = 0.0003, alpha +6.6% p.a. at t = 3.35 surviving a hostile long-short momentum control, and positive in every one of eleven regime cuts. And 6-1 momentum at H=63: alpha +15.1% p.a. at t = 5.81, net +11.4% after 40 bps, PBO 0.000 on horizon choice.

E · Strongest contradictory evidence

Two things cut against the headline. First, the momentum edge has decayed sharply in the most recent third — 6-1 momentum is ~0% at H=21 since January 2024 against +7.0% in the middle third — which is exactly the post-publication decay pattern McLean & Pontiff document, and the engine’s own decay monitor already flags. Second, the composite that fails on the full sample is positive and net-positive over the same recent window (+3.6% net at H=5, gross Sharpe 1.06), driven by a delivery theme that has no analogue outside India and no significant alpha over the full sample. A verdict of "the composite doesn’t work" is a full-sample verdict, and the recent sample disagrees with it.

F · What does not work

Everything under 5 sessions after costs. The composite as a long book, full-sample. The composite in downtrends, high volatility and the liquid tercile. The significance gate as a binary. The score as a probability. Value and quality, which are not measured at all. Delivery as an alpha source after controls, though not as a factor with information.

G · What survives realistic transaction costs

Under the base 40 bps scenario: 6-1 momentum long-short at H≥5 (+3.1% to +11.7%), residual momentum long-short at H≥5 (+1.5% to +6.6%), the equal-weight family composite long at H≥5 (+4.7% to +8.7%), and the 5-session return-target refit at H≥10. Under the conservative 70 bps scenario the first two survive from H=10 and H=21 respectively. Under the stressed 120 bps scenario only H≥42 survives for anything. The shipped composite survives no scenario at any horizon on the full sample.

H · Recommended production holding period

21 sessions as the operating point, with a 63-session tolerance band. 21 gives 91 independent observations a year to monitor with, sits inside the net plateau, and reduces the tail dependence that concentrates at longer horizons. Do not run below 5. Do not run above 126 without accepting that you can no longer measure whether it is working.

I · Recommended signal refresh frequency

Score daily; act on a 21-session cadence with a buffer band. Daily scoring costs nothing and the persistence data supports it: a top-decile name has a 90.5% chance of still being top-decile tomorrow and 58.3% in 21 sessions, so a hysteresis band converts a daily score into ~0.48 book turnover per rebalance rather than 30 turns a year. Stale signals should be retained inside the band and dropped when they leave the top three deciles, not when they leave the top one.

J · Should multiple horizons coexist?

Yes — but as separately sized sleeves, not as one blended score. The factors demonstrably have different clocks (reversal crosses sign between 42 and 63 sessions; delivery peaks at four days; momentum accrues for a year). But the measured blend test is a warning: a 50/50 fast/slow blend returned +3.0% net at H=63 against +11.7% for the slow sleeve alone, because the fast sleeve’s turnover consumed the book. Coexistence has to be at the portfolio level with separate capital and separate cost budgets.

K · The experiment that would most reduce uncertainty

The parallel rank-target versus return-target forward run described in §14. Second priority: extend the delivery series backwards, or accept that the largest live coefficient is fitted on 75 of 83 cross-sections and size it accordingly.

L · Final verdict

ProSignal is a competently constructed cross-sectional momentum harvester whose factor library contains genuine, deflation-resistant, cost-surviving information at 10–126 sessions — and whose fitting layer is currently the weakest component in the system rather than the strongest. The horizon question has a clear answer and it is not the interesting one: 21–63 sessions, on a broad plateau, close to what the engine already runs. The interesting answer is that the engine’s edge is being lost between the factors and the score, not between the score and the market. An unweighted average of its own seven columns beats its fitted composite on every economic measure at every horizon; a single one of its own factors, used raw, beats it by 15 percentage points of annualised long-short alpha.

That is a good problem to have. The information is there, it is measurable, and it is upstream of the part that is broken.

Method notes, limitations and literature

Evidence tiers

Kept separate throughout, as required.

Observed — what the data literally shows Significant — survives overlap-corrected inference Robust — survives alternative periods, regimes and specifications Economic — survives turnover and costs Mechanism — has a credible economic story

What this study could not do

  • No intraday data exists in the store. The shortest measurable horizon is one session, close to close. Anything about same-day or intraday behaviour is unanswerable here and is not answered.
  • Market capitalisation is largely unavailable. Shares outstanding cover 186 symbols in the fundamentals file and 758 in the statements file from 2023-06. Size analysis therefore uses 60-session median traded value as the size and liquidity proxy, which is what the universe screen itself uses. Conclusions about "small caps" are conclusions about thinly-traded names.
  • The admission mask is not applied. The engine restricts training to names above their own ATR invalidation level; that predicate needs exit rules this study did not reimplement. The panel here is therefore ~4% larger than the engine’s (45,164 rows against 43,555 on the matched 83 dates). This runs in the direction of a wider, less-flattering population.
  • Universe history is bounded externally. NSE archives return 403 before 2016-01; the panel cannot start before 2018-11 given the 756-session residual window and the 300-session listing requirement. Independent observations at H=189 number ten. No amount of methodology fixes that.
  • The recent/full split was chosen after seeing the full-sample result, using a pre-defined equal-thirds structure. It is reported as a hypothesis, not a test.
  • Impact costs are parameterised, not calibrated. The engine’s own config marks the impact coefficient UNVALIDATED. The four scenarios bracket plausible values; none is fitted to realised slippage, because none exists.

Where this sits against published evidence

  • Consistent with the literature. The rising-IC / flat-return horizon profile, the 1–21 session reversal crossing into 63+ session momentum, and the front-loaded decay of residual momentum are the standard cross-sectional pattern (Jegadeesh & Titman 1993; Lehmann 1990; George & Hwang 2004). Residual momentum outperforming raw momentum on a risk-adjusted basis and avoiding the downtrend crash is precisely the Blitz, Huij & Martens (2011) result, reproduced here out of sample on Indian data. The lottery block’s negative short-horizon IC matches Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw (2011), stronger here than in US samples — consistent with a retail-dominated marginal buyer.
  • The decay is expected, not anomalous. McLean & Pontiff (2016) put post-publication decay at ~58% of in-sample return; the engine’s own decay monitor already applies that haircut. The weakening of momentum in the recent third is inside that envelope and should not be read as the signal breaking.
  • The IC-versus-net-return divergence is a known trap. Qian, Sorensen & Hua’s work on information horizon and turnover makes the same point: the horizon that maximises forecast quality is systematically longer than the one that maximises net alpha, and choosing on IC alone will always over-extend the holding period.
  • Potentially local, not novel. The delivery-percentage factor has no clean analogue outside India. It carries the highest t-statistic in the live model and no alpha after standard controls. The honest classification is "an India-specific liquidity/conviction proxy whose incremental value is not yet demonstrated", not "a discovered factor".
  • The rank-target finding is not, as far as this study can tell, standard practice being violated — rank targets are common and defensible for robustness. What is specific here is the interaction with a cross-sectional skew of +1.9 to +4.9, which is high even for an emerging market, and which makes the median-ordering objective unusually costly.
  • Deflation and overfitting methodology follows Bailey & López de Prado (deflated Sharpe ratio; probability of backtest overfitting via combinatorially symmetric cross-validation) and López de Prado’s purging/embargo scheme, which the engine already implements in validation/cpcv.py.
